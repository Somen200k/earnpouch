If a job ad sends you to NewJobsLooker, it is reasonable to ask: **Is this site an employer, a job-search service, or a scam?** The campaign link on this page leads to a third-party job-search service. EarnPouch cannot certify the service's safety or verify every job it may display. Use it, if you choose, as a place to discover listings—not as confirmation that an employer is hiring or that a job is right for you.

Before you enter a ZIP code or contact details, check who operates the site, how it uses information, where each listing leads, and whether the job appears on the employer's own careers site.

## What is NewJobsLooker?

NewJobsLooker presents itself as a job-search service. A job-search aggregator or discovery service may show openings from employers, recruiters, or other sources, and may send visitors to another website to view details or apply. The exact sources, filters, and application flow can vary.

Do not assume every result is supplied directly by an employer, that every listing is current, or that searching on an aggregator completes an application. Check the listing's named employer, original source, date, location, and application destination. If those details are not clear, find the role on the employer's official careers website before sharing an application.

## Is NewJobsLooker legit or a scam?

EarnPouch has not independently audited NewJobsLooker's ownership, security, listing sources, privacy practices, or every page in its redirect flow. For that reason, we cannot give the service a blanket safety endorsement or guarantee that each listing is legitimate. A third-party website may help with discovery while still requiring you to verify listings and carefully review how your information is used.

Before using the service:

- Read its current terms of use and privacy notice.
- Check the organization or contact details it provides.
- Notice whether a link redirects to a recruiter, employer, advertiser, or another service.
- Confirm that the named job exists on the employer's official careers site.
- Stop if the page makes unrealistic hiring promises or requests unexpected payment or sensitive account credentials.

Website reputation scores from external services can change and use different criteria. A score alone does not establish that a job listing is real, that the site is safe for your specific use, or that an employer is affiliated with the service.

## Why might a job-search service ask for a ZIP code?

A location can help a search service display nearby results, but a form's purpose and the handling of submitted information are controlled by the service's disclosures. Review the privacy notice before entering a ZIP code, email address, phone number, or availability. Check whether the information is required, whether it may be shared with partners, and whether submitting it signs you up for messages.

Do not assume NewJobsLooker's filters include immediate-start dates, shift preferences, or any other feature unless the live interface clearly provides it. The available searches and results may change.

## How to verify a listing before applying

1. **Identify the actual employer.** Look for the company's name, work location, and source of the listing.
2. **Check whether the posting is current.** Compare its title and location with the employer's careers site.
3. **Review the details.** Confirm duties, schedule, qualifications, and application steps in the employer's own posting.
4. **Check the destination.** Know whether the application goes to the employer, a recruiter, or a separate third-party service.
5. **Apply through a verified route.** If the role is listed on the employer's official careers site, follow those instructions and retain the job title or requisition number.

For Walmart openings, you can search [Walmart Careers](https://careers.walmart.com/) directly. Third-party listings are not a substitute for the employer's current description or application portal.

## Job-search scam warning signs

- A request to pay an application, interview, placement, equipment, or onboarding fee before you can get a job.
- A promise of guaranteed employment without a normal application or screening process.
- A request for bank login details, passwords, payment-card PINs, or one-time security codes.
- An unexpected message asking you to move to a private messaging app or provide sensitive information before you can verify the recruiter.
- Job duties, pay, employer identity, or work location that change when you follow the link.

Verify recruiters using contact information found on the employer's official website. Never send money or sensitive information because a listing or message uses a familiar company name.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does NewJobsLooker hire applicants?

NewJobsLooker is presented as a job-search service, not the employer for every listing. Check the individual posting to identify who is hiring and where the application goes.

### Are jobs found there guaranteed to be real and available?

No. Listings can be outdated, incomplete, or lead to another site. Verify the opening on the employer's official careers site before applying.

### Does the service charge job seekers?

Check the current terms and any page you are asked to use. Be cautious of payment requests to apply, secure an interview, or obtain a job; verify them with the employer before proceeding.

### Is it safe to enter my ZIP code?

That depends on the site's privacy practices and your comfort with them. Read the privacy notice and form disclosures first, and share only information you are comfortable providing.

### Does using an aggregator guarantee an interview or job?

No. A search, click, or application does not guarantee an interview, job offer, or employment.
