A promotion advertising a $750 PayPal reward is enough to make anyone pause. That skepticism is useful: the headline describes a **chance for a reward**, not a guaranteed instant transfer, and it doesn’t tell you by itself who operates the campaign.

The offer details supplied for this page refer to sponsor-deal requirements. The live rules—not a headline or social post—should explain exactly what participation involves, who is eligible, and whether any reward is available.

## How this promotion may work

This is a third-party campaign, separate from PayPal’s financial services. The supplied offer information describes a reward path involving sponsor deals, but EarnPouch has not independently verified the current deal list, completion requirements, or costs.

Before starting, find out whether the campaign requires a particular number or type of activities, whether any activity involves a paid subscription or purchase, and what proof of completion is needed. Don’t assume that registration alone qualifies you for the $750 reward.

## What to check in the fine print

Before entering an email address or other information, look for:

- **The organizer:** Find the sponsor and administrator named in the official rules. A PayPal name or logo alone doesn’t show that PayPal runs the promotion.
- **Eligibility:** Confirm eligible locations, age limits, deadlines, and exclusions in the current rules.
- **Required activities:** Check which sponsor deals are mandatory, whether they have separate terms, and whether any charge or subscription may apply.
- **Reward conditions:** Look for the exact reward, completion thresholds, verification process, and delivery terms.
- **Privacy and consent:** Understand who receives your information, how it may be used, and whether you’re opting into marketing.

If the rules are missing, unclear, or different from the offer page, pause. Never share your PayPal password, bank login, payment-card PIN, or one-time security code to participate.

## Watch for payment scams

Be wary of anyone who says you must send money to release a prize. The FTC warns that requests to pay with gift cards are a scam sign; see its guide to [gift card scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/gift-card-scams). Don’t pay an unexpected “clearance” or “release” fee, and verify the organizer independently.
