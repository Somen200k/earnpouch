An online promotion advertising a $750 Walmart reward can sound tempting—and it’s sensible to ask what’s required. The supplied offer description refers to sponsor activities, but it doesn’t establish the exact steps, any costs, or whether Walmart is involved.

Before you share information or sign up for anything, check the live campaign page and official rules. They should identify the organizer, explain eligibility, and spell out what you must do and how the reward is delivered.

## How the promotion may work

This is a third-party campaign, separate from Walmart’s retail services. The supplied draft describes a reward program involving sponsor deals, but EarnPouch has not independently verified the current deal list, completion thresholds, or any associated costs.

Read each offer’s terms before starting. Check whether a deal involves a subscription, purchase, or trial that may renew, and how the campaign confirms completion. Don’t assume a particular number of tasks is required or that completing them guarantees the advertised reward unless the official rules clearly say so.

## What to verify in the fine print

- **Organizer and sponsor:** Find their names in the official rules. Walmart’s name or logo alone doesn’t show Walmart runs the campaign.
- **Eligibility:** Confirm eligible locations, age limits, deadlines, and any device restrictions in the live rules.
- **Required sponsor activities:** Check which activities are required, whether they involve costs or subscriptions, and how completion is tracked.
- **Reward conditions:** Look for the exact prize, any thresholds, the selection or fulfillment process, and how a participant is notified.
- **Privacy and consent:** Understand who collects your information and whether it may be shared with partners or used for marketing.

If key details are missing or unclear, pause rather than submitting information. Never provide account passwords, bank logins, payment-card PINs, or one-time security codes to enter or claim a promotion.

## Watch for payment scams

Be wary of anyone who asks you to pay an unexpected fee to claim a prize. The FTC warns that requests to pay with gift cards are a scam sign; see its guide to [gift card scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/gift-card-scams). Verify the organizer using contact information you find independently.
