## Sweepstakes vs. lottery: what is the difference?

In general, a sweepstakes awards a prize based on chance and must provide a lawful way to enter without paying or making a purchase. A lottery typically combines a prize, chance, and consideration (such as payment to enter) and is generally restricted to government-run or specially authorized operators. Laws and definitions vary by jurisdiction, so this is only a general explanation—not legal advice. Check the promotion's official rules and applicable local requirements.

If an entry page or message asks you to pay an entry fee, buy something to claim a prize, or pay unexpected shipping or processing costs, stop and verify the promotion with the organizer using contact information from its official site.

## Prize taxes and IRS reporting

Prize winnings may have tax consequences. The IRS's [2026 Instructions for Form 1099-MISC](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099mec) list a $2,000 reporting threshold for prizes and awards for payments made after December 31, 2025. The threshold concerns information reporting; it does not, by itself, determine whether a prize is taxable. Tax treatment can depend on the facts and applicable federal and state rules. A $1,000 advertised prize does not automatically mean the organizer must issue a Form 1099-MISC, nor does the absence of a form necessarily settle the recipient's tax obligations. Consult the IRS or a qualified tax professional for advice about your circumstances.

## Protect yourself from giveaway scams

- **Do not pay to claim a prize.** Be suspicious of unexpected requests for taxes, processing, shipping, or release fees paid directly to a sender.
- **Do not share financial credentials.** An entry or claim should not require your bank password, payment-card PIN, account password, or one-time security code.
- **Verify the sender.** Compare a winner notice with the official rules and use contact information published on the organizer's official website.
- **Check the sponsor.** A brand name or logo in a promotion does not prove that the brand sponsors or endorses it.
- **Read the privacy terms.** Understand how your email and other information may be used before submitting the form.

If the sponsor, administrator, rules, or privacy notice are unclear or inconsistent, do not submit your information.
